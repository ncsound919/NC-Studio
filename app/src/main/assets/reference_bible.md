# 1. ENGINEER PROFILES & SIGNATURE TECHNIQUES

## 1.1 Dr. Dre — G-Funk / West Coast (The Chronic, 2001)
**Gear & workflow:** MPC60 + Roland Juno 60 + Minimoog; recorded at Death Row/Can-Am; mixed on an **SSL console**; consistent monitoring rig (Auratone/Yamaha NS-10 + main monitors) carried from studio to studio. Collaborators: Chris "The Glove" Taylor (mix), Colin Wolfe (live bass/guitar/flute), Bernie Grundman (mastering).

**Signature approach:**
- **Sample sparingly** — one or few samples per song (a drum beat or a bass melody), then **complete with live instrumentation** (flute, guitar, bass). Unlike The Bomb Squad's dense collage, Dre leaves space.
- **G-funk = slow, rolling bass + squealing synth leads + soulful backing vocals.** Moog provides the "funky worm" melodies and low-end; Juno 60 provides pads/strings.
- **Perfectionist fine-tuning** — every sound tweaked to exact taste; mixes are "the cleanest and fullest" by obsessive iteration.
- **Snare reverb trick:** kick and bass are dead silent for the exact length of the snare's reverb tail — the reverb fills the void left by the absent low end, so the snare feels huge without low-end mud.
- **Monitoring discipline:** same nearfields everywhere so decisions translate.
- **Vocal placement:** upfront but never harsh; de-essed, with air but controlled sibilance.

**What to steal:** One dominant sample + live overdubs; sub-bass in mono; sidechain-style space for the snare tail; consistent monitoring.

---

## 1.2 Mark "Spike" Stent — Pop / Rock / R&B (Flowers, As It Was, Lemonade)
**Workflow:** Starts **drums → bass → vocals → key hooks**, then pulls vocals out to fine-tune guitars/parts. Moved from SSL G-series to **in-the-box for urban/pop**, analog desk for rock/acoustic. Assistant (Matty) cleans/organizes and preps samples.

**Signature techniques:**
- **Sub-grouping everything** — 4 kick tracks with effects on each, summed and compressed as a group; toms cleaned of spill/ring; samples added to toms for punch.
- **Kick recipe:** HPF rumble → boost ~100 Hz (weight) + 4–5 kHz (attack) → parallel compression for sustain → subtle distortion for presence.
- **SSL bus compression** for "glue" on the mix bus.
- **Vocal presence** maintained by **aggressive midrange EQ cuts on guitars** (carve 1–3 kHz) so vocals cut without raising their level.
- **Parallel compression** on drums for stadium power; multi-layered guitars panned precisely for wall-of-sound width.
- **Serves the song** — no imposed signature sound; emotion over perfection.

**What to steal:** Sub-group + group compression; carve guitar mids for vocals; parallel drum compression; tom spill cleanup.

---

## 1.3 Andy Wallace — Rock (Smells Like Teen Spirit, Reign in Blood, Evil Empire)
**Philosophy:** Uses **the same EQ and compressor on every track in a mix** — consistency over variety. Maximize dynamics while controlling them; make drums punch and guitars cut without mud.

**Signature approach:**
- Minimal processing variety — one go-to EQ/comp per track type, dialed per source.
- **Drum focus:** snare crack and transient punch are paramount; kick tight and defined.
- **Guitars:** layered and panned, with low-mid control to avoid masking vocals.
- **Vocals:** sit clearly above distorted guitars via arrangement and EQ, not just level.
- **Dynamic contrast:** verses restrained, choruses explosive — don't compress the life out.

**What to steal:** Pick one EQ/comp and master it; prioritize snare transient; preserve verse/chorus dynamics.

---

## 1.4 StoneBridge (Sten Hallström) — House (Show Me Love)
**Signature:** Organ bass (Korg M1 "Organ 2"), sidechain ducking, vocal chopped/stuttered above the groove. Mix by StoneBridge + P. Dennis Mitchell + Junior Vasquez.

**What to steal:** Sidechain the bass/pads to the kick; keep vocal on top; HPF hats aggressively; wide pads, mono sub.

# 2. GENRE MIXING PLAYBOOKS

## 2.1 Classic Hip-Hop / G-Funk
- **Tempo:** 85–100 BPM (half-time feel over 16th hats).
- **Low end:** sub-bass in mono, 40–60 Hz fundamental, warm and round (Moog). Kick and bass must not fight — sidechain or tune.
- **Synths:** Moog lead (saw, filter env, portamento, vibrato) + Juno pads/strings (chorus II). P-Funk sample for groove.
- **Drums:** 808 kick or sampled kick; snare with reverb tail filling the void; hi-hats light.
- **Vocals:** upfront, de-essed, slight saturation; backing vocals wide (harmonies panned).
- **Space:** reverb on snare only; keep bass dry; tape saturation on mix bus.
- **G-funk trick:** one sample + live overdubs; snare reverb fills the gap when kick/bass duck.

## 2.2 Pop (Modern, Stent-style)
- **Tempo:** 100–130 BPM.
- **Low end:** tight, controlled, HPF everything below 30 Hz except kick/sub.
- **Drums:** layered (acoustic + samples), parallel compression, tom spill cleaned, kick boosted 100 Hz + 4–5 kHz.
- **Vocals:** upfront, de-essed (5–8 kHz), air shelf 10–12 kHz, saturation, doubles/harmonies panned wide.
- **Width:** guitars/keys panned L/R; vocals center; bass/kick mono.
- **Glue:** SSL bus compression; subtle tape saturation.
- **Loudness:** ~-8 to -6 LUFS integrated, true peak ≤ -1 dBTP.
- **Trick:** carve 1–3 kHz on guitars to make room for vocals; sub-group and compress.

## 2.3 House (Classic)
- **Tempo:** 120–130 BPM, four-on-the-floor.
- **Kick:** tuned, punchy, HPF <30 Hz, boost 60–80 Hz (weight) + 3–5 kHz (click). Mono.
- **Bass:** organ/synth (Juno), sidechained to kick (4:1, fast attack, fast release, or LFO pump).
- **Hats:** off-beat, HPF 300–500 Hz, boost 8–10 kHz; open hat on "and" of 4.
- **Snare/Clap:** on 2 & 4, short plate reverb (1.0–1.2 s), HPF 200 Hz.
- **Pads:** wide, sidechained to kick, reverb/delay.
- **Vocals:** chopped/stuttered, upfront, often filtered (LPF sweeps).
- **Trick:** sidechain pump is the genre's heartbeat; keep sub mono; reference on club system.

## 2.4 Trap / Drill
- **Tempo:** 130–160 BPM (half-time feel).
- **808:** tuned to key, glide/portamento, decay medium-long, distorted/saturated, sidechained to kick (2:1–4:1), HPF 25–30 Hz, boost 50–80 Hz, cut 200–400 Hz (mud), parallel distortion layer panned L/R slightly detuned.
- **Hi-hats:** rolling 32nd/64th, pitch variation per hit, ghost notes, velocity ramp on rolls, HPF 200–400 Hz, boost 8–12 kHz.
- **Snare:** layered (acoustic + clap + 808 rim), short plate reverb, boost 200 Hz (body) + 5 kHz (crack).
- **Melody:** sparse, dark, minor, often a single loop; vocal Auto-Tune heavy (hard tuning, retune speed 0).
- **Space:** reverb on snare/vocals; bass dry; wide stereo on hats/perc.
- **Drill (Brooklyn/UK):** darker, more aggressive 808s, sliding bass, eerie synths, faster tempos (140+), sliding 808 glide.
- **Trick:** separate 808 transient (kick) from tail (sub); tune 808 to root; velocity variation on hats.

## 2.5 R&B
- **Tempo:** 60–90 BPM (slow jams) or 90–110 (mid).
- **Drums:** soft kicks (controlled low end), crisp snares with room reverb, hi-hat rolls that breathe; drums support vocal.
- **Bass:** warm, round, locks with kick, sub controlled, sits in the pocket.
- **Vocals:** stacked harmonies (3-part), doubles panned wide, vocal chops/pads/strings/guitar licks as ear candy.
- **Modern R&B EQ:** more low and high end, scooped mids (careful — can sound brittle); high shelf on vocals/chords; cut lows on non-bass tracks.
- **Vocal chain:** HPF 80–100 Hz, de-ess, comp 2:1–3:1, saturation, air shelf 10–12 kHz, delay throws (filtered), plate reverb.
- **Trick:** sidechain bass to kick; reverb on snares for space; harmonies spread across stereo field.

## 2.6 Gospel
- **Tempo:** 60–120 BPM (varies: ballads to upbeat).
- **Voices first:** lead vocal forward and relatively dry; choir wide and deep (reverb) behind; depth, not level, separates them.
- **Dynamics:** preserve live-performance dynamics — don't over-compress into consistency.
- **Lead vs. choir:** sidechain EQ — duck accompaniment for vocals; big hall reverb on vocals/choir.
- **Panning "vocal sandwich":** lead center, doubles/harmonies panned hard L/R; mid-side EQ/compression to widen choir.
- **Instrumentation:** organ, piano, bass (warm), live drums (not too tight), tambourine/shaker for groove.
- **Trick:** modulate up a half/whole step for the final chorus; vamp/outro with ad-libs; automate for builds.

## 2.7 Rock
- **Tempo:** 100–160 BPM.
- **Drums:** punchy kick/snare, toms cleaned of spill/ring, parallel compression, samples layered for consistency.
- **Guitars:** wall-of-sound via precise panning; aggressive midrange cuts (1–3 kHz) to make room for vocals; HPF 80–120 Hz, boost 2–4 kHz presence.
- **Bass:** tight, defined under heavy guitars, HPF 30–40 Hz, saturation.
- **Vocals:** upfront, de-essed, slapback delay (80–120 ms) optional, plate reverb.
- **Space:** room/plate reverb on snare; keep guitars relatively dry.
- **Glue:** SSL bus compression; tape saturation.
- **Trick (Wallace):** same EQ/comp on every track; preserve verse/chorus dynamics; snare transient is king.

# 3. INSTRUMENT MIX PROFILES

## Kick
- HPF 25–35 Hz (remove rumble).
- Boost 60–80 Hz (weight/thump), 3–5 kHz (attack/click).
- Cut 250–400 Hz (mud/boxiness).
- Parallel compression (4:1–8:1, fast attack, medium release), blend 30–50%.
- Saturation for presence; tune to key (trap/808).
- Keep mono; check on small speakers.

## Snare
- HPF 100–150 Hz.
- Boost 200 Hz (body), 5 kHz (crack), cut 400–800 Hz (boxiness).
- Reverb: plate, 1.0–1.5 s, pre-delay 20–40 ms; or gated (80s).
- Parallel compression; layer acoustic + clap + rim (trap).
- Clean tom spill; tune snare to key if needed.

## 808 / Sub
- Tune to track key (root); use glide/portamento (trap).
- HPF 25–30 Hz; boost 50–80 Hz; cut 200–400 Hz.
- Sidechain to kick (2:1–4:1, medium-fast attack, fast release).
- Distortion/saturation for grit; parallel distortion layer panned L/R, slightly detuned.
- Separate transient (kick) from tail (sub); mono below 120 Hz.
- Check phase with kick; avoid 808 masking kick.

## Bass (Melodic/Synth)
- HPF 30–40 Hz; boost 80–120 Hz (warmth); cut 250–500 Hz (mud).
- Saturation/tape; sidechain to kick.
- Mono below 100–120 Hz; width above via chorus/delay.
- Lock timing with kick (tight pocket).

## Vocals (Lead)
- HPF 80–120 Hz; de-ess 5–8 kHz (narrow, 2–4 dB).
- Compress 2:1–4:1, medium attack (10–30 ms), fast release (50–100 ms) or auto.
- Saturation (tube) for warmth; air shelf 10–12 kHz (+1–3 dB).
- Delay: 1/8 or 1/4, filtered (LPF 3–4 kHz), feedback 20–30%.
- Reverb: plate/hall, pre-delay 20–40 ms, 1.2–2.0 s (gospel: longer hall).
- Doubles/harmonies panned L/R; lead center.

## Guitars
- HPF 80–120 Hz; cut 250–500 Hz (mud); boost 2–4 kHz (presence), 8–10 kHz (air).
- Pan L/R for width; cut 1–3 kHz to make room for vocals (Stent).
- Parallel distortion for crunch; keep relatively dry (rock).
- Stereo widening via double-tracking, not just panning.

## Keys / Pads / Synths
- HPF 100–200 Hz; cut 300–500 Hz; boost 5–8 kHz (air).
- Sidechain to vocals (duck 2–3 dB).
- Stereo width: chorus, haas delay (10–20 ms), or mid-side.
- Reverb/delay for depth; automate filter sweeps for builds.

## Hi-Hats
- HPF 200–400 Hz; boost 8–12 kHz (crispness).
- Pan slightly off-center; velocity variation (don't quantize velocity flat).
- Trap: pitch variation per hit, ghost notes, rolls with velocity ramp.
- House: off-beat pattern, open hat on "and" of 4.
- Short reverb or none; keep tight.

# 4. DRUM PRESETS

## 4.1 Classic Roland TR-808 Kick
- **Waveform:** sine (pitched).
- **Fundamental:** 50–60 Hz (tune to key).
- **Decay:** 1.0–2.0 s (long, boomy).
- **Tone (click):** high (adds transient).
- **Level:** high; accent for variation.
- **Mix:** HPF 25 Hz, boost 50 Hz, sidechain tail; keep mono; layer with acoustic kick transient for punch.

## 4.2 Trap 808
- **Source:** TR-808 kick sample, tuned to key (root).
- **Decay:** medium (0.5–1.0 s) for punch, or long for sub.
- **Glide/Portamento:** 100–300 ms (sliding 808s).
- **Saturation:** heavy (tube/transistor) for grit; parallel distortion.
- **Layering:** acoustic kick transient (HPF 100 Hz) for click; 808 for sub.
- **Sidechain:** to kick (2:1–4:1).
- **Pitch variation:** slide up/down a minor 3rd or octave for fills.
- **Pan:** mono sub; distortion layer slightly L/R.

## 4.3 Boom Bap Kick
- **Source:** sampled acoustic kick from drum break (e.g., "Impeach the President," "Amen Brother").
- **Trim:** tight start, short decay (no ring).
- **Pitch:** match to track (±1–2 semitones).
- **Processing:** parallel compression (NY), slight saturation, EQ (boost 60 Hz, cut 300 Hz).
- **Avoid:** kicks with hi-hat bleed; match kick/snare sample lengths for coherence.

## 4.4 Boom Bap Snare
- **Source:** sampled acoustic snare from break.
- **Trim:** tight, no tail (or short tail).
- **Layer:** add clap or rimshot for crack.
- **Processing:** boost 200 Hz + 5 kHz, cut 400–600 Hz, parallel comp, short plate reverb.
- **Swing:** 55–65% (MPC swing).

## 4.5 Hi-Hat Patterns
- **Trap:** 16th/32nd rolls; open hat on off-beats; pitch variation per hit; ghost notes; velocity ramp on rolls (quiet → loud); pan subtle L/R.
- **Boom Bap:** swung 16ths; open hat occasional; velocity humanized.
- **House:** closed hat on every 8th; open hat on "and" of 4; off-beat pattern.
- **Processing:** HPF 200–400 Hz, boost 8–12 kHz, pan, velocity variation, short reverb or none.

## 4.6 Snare (Trap)
- **Layers:** acoustic snare + clap + 808 rim/click.
- **Reverb:** short plate (0.6–1.0 s), pre-delay 10 ms.
- **EQ:** boost 200 Hz (body), 5 kHz (crack); cut 400–800 Hz.
- **Parallel compression** for punch.

# 5. CLASSIC SYNTH SETTINGS

## 5.1 Roland Juno (60/106) — Architecture
- **DCO:** saw / square / pulse (+ sub oscillator -1 octave, + noise).
- **HPF:** 0–3 (high-pass filter).
- **VCF:** LPF with resonance (0–100), env amount (±).
- **VCA env:** ADSR (attack, decay, sustain, release).
- **LFO:** rate, delay; modulates pitch/filter/PWM.
- **Chorus:** I / II / off (stereo widening).
- **PWM:** pulse width + LFO modulation.

### Juno Patch Recipes

**A. G-Funk / West Coast Lead (Moog-style on Juno)**
- DCO: saw + sub; PWM 50%.
- HPF: 0.
- VCF cutoff: 40%; resonance: 30%; env amount: +60%.
- Env: attack 20 ms, decay 300 ms, sustain 40%, release 200 ms.
- LFO: rate 5 Hz, pitch mod small (vibrato), delay 0.5 s.
- Chorus: I.
- Portamento: on (medium), for sliding leads.
- Mix: mono or slight stereo; reverb short; delay filtered.

**B. Juno Brass**
- DCO: saw.
- VCF cutoff: 50%; resonance: 20%; env amount: +50%.
- Env: attack 50 ms, decay 400 ms, sustain 60%, release 300 ms.
- Chorus: I.
- Filter: gentle LPF; boost 2–4 kHz for presence.

**C. Juno Strings / Pad**
- DCO: saw + square; sub on.
- VCF cutoff: 60%; resonance: 10%; env amount: +30%.
- Env: attack 800 ms–1.2 s, decay 500 ms, sustain 80%, release 1.5 s.
- LFO: rate 4–6 Hz, pitch mod small (chorus-like detune).
- Chorus: II.
- Mix: wide stereo; reverb medium (1.5–2.0 s); sidechain to kick.

**D. Juno Bass**
- DCO: square or saw + sub.
- HPF: 0.
- VCF cutoff: 25–35%; resonance: 40–60%; env amount: +70%.
- Env: attack 5 ms, decay 200 ms, sustain 20%, release 100 ms.
- Chorus: off (or I subtle).
- Mix: mono; HPF 30 Hz; boost 80 Hz; sidechain to kick.

**E. Juno Pad (Ambient/Pop)**
- DCO: saw + square, PWM modulated.
- VCF cutoff: 50%; resonance: 15%; env amount: +40%.
- Env: attack 1 s, decay 600 ms, sustain 70%, release 2 s.
- LFO: rate 0.5–1 Hz, PWM mod, filter mod small.
- Chorus: II.
- Mix: wide; reverb long; sidechain to vocals.

**F. Juno Arp / Sequence**
- DCO: square.
- VCF cutoff: 45%; resonance: 35%; env amount: +60%.
- Env: attack 5 ms, decay 150 ms, sustain 0%, release 50 ms.
- LFO: rate 8–12 Hz, filter mod.
- Chorus: I.

**G. Acid Bass (303-style on Juno)**
- DCO: saw.
- VCF cutoff: very low (23); resonance: very high (204).
- Env amount: high positive (classic 303 resonant sweep).
- Effect 1: bit-crusher for "robot vowel bass."
- Env: attack 5 ms, decay 200 ms, sustain 0%, release 100 ms.

**H. Reese Bass (DnB/House)**
- DCO: saw (detuned oscillators).
- VCF cutoff: ~130 (filtered); resonance: medium.
- Keep filter params otherwise default; play low on keyboard.
- Mix: heavy filtering, reverb/delay.

### Juno Universal Starting Point
- DCO: saw, sub on, PWM 50%.
- HPF: 0.
- VCF cutoff: 40%; resonance: 30%; env amount: 60%.
- Env: A 20 ms, D 300 ms, S 40%, R 200 ms.
- LFO: rate 5 Hz, pitch mod small.
- Chorus: II.

## 5.2 Yamaha DX7 — Architecture
- **6 operators** (sinusoidal), configurable in **32 algorithms** (series/parallel routing).
- **Per operator:** ratio/frequency, level, envelope (4-stage rate/level: R1-L1-R2-L2-R3-L3-R4-L4), velocity sensitivity, key scaling.
- **Feedback** on operator 6 (for brightness/noise).
- **LFO:** rate, delay, pitch/amp/filter mod, waveform.
- **Pitch envelope:** rate/level (4-stage).
- **Output:** algorithm determines carrier/modulator relationships.

### DX7 Classic Patch Recipes

**Algorithm selection guide:**
- **Algorithm 1 (all series):** bright, complex, good for bells/leads.
- **Algorithm 5 (parallel carriers 1&2, modulated by 3&4, 5&6):** EP, strings, pads — the "classic DX7" sound.
- **Algorithm 16 (3 parallel pairs):** organ, brass.
- **Algorithm 32 (all parallel):** additive, organ-like.

**A. E.Piano 1 (Preset 11) — The Twin Peaks Sound**
- Algorithm: 5.
- Operators 1 & 2: carriers, ratio 1:1, level high (99/85).
- Operator 2 (modulator): fast attack, medium decay, velocity sensitivity high.
- Feedback: 5 (on op 6) for brightness.
- Envelope: sharp attack (bell), medium decay, low sustain, medium release.
- Mix: chorus + delay + reverb (wet) to come alive; velocity-sensitive.

**B. Bass 1 (Preset 15) — THE 80s Bass**
- Algorithm: 1 or 5.
- Low operators (ratio 1:1 or 2:1), fast attack, short decay, low sustain.
- Level: high on carriers, medium on modulators.
- Feedback: low-medium.
- Mix: dry, HPF 30 Hz, boost 80 Hz; sidechain to kick.

**C. Fretless Bass (Preset 16 / Bass 2)**
- Algorithm: 5.
- Smoother envelope (longer decay/sustain), softer attack.
- Used: Berlin "Take My Breath Away," Commodores "Night Shift."
- Mix: chorus + reverb (wet).

**D. Brass 1 (Preset 1)**
- Algorithm: 5 or 16.
- Bright modulators, medium attack, medium sustain.
- Velocity sensitivity: high (breath controller feel).
- Mix: reverb medium; slight chorus.

**E. Strings 1 (Preset 4)**
- Algorithm: 5.
- Glassy, fast attack, long sustain, slow release.
- Mix: chorus + reverb; layer with analog strings for warmth.

**F. Syn-Lead 1 (Preset 14)**
- Algorithm: 1 or 5.
- Quasi-analog solo; medium attack, high sustain.
- Portamento on.
- Mix: delay + reverb; chorus.

**G. Harmonica (ROM2-A Preset 18)**
- Algorithm: 5.
- Used in Tina Turner "What's Love Got To Do With It."
- Bright, breathy, velocity-sensitive.

**H. Tubular Bells**
- Algorithm: 1 (series) for inharmonic partials.
- Fast attack, long decay, no sustain, high feedback.
- Mix: reverb long, chorus.

**I. Dire, Dire Docks (Mario 64 water theme)**
- Based on E.Piano 1 with subtle operator changes for brightness; chorus + delay + reverb (wet).

### DX7 Universal Tips
- **Always add effects** — chorus, delay, reverb — digital FM sounds cold/dry without them.
- **Velocity sensitivity** is key to expressive patches.
- **Feedback** (op 6) adds brightness/noise — use sparingly (0–7).
- **Key scaling** controls brightness across the keyboard (higher notes brighter).
- **Pitch envelope** creates the "attack transient" (bell-like) on EPs.
- **Don't over-program** — the factory presets defined the 80s sound; start from them.

# 6. SESSION / BUS TEMPLATES

## 6.1 Standard Mix Bus Routing
```
Tracks → Instrument Group Buses → Mix Bus → Master
```
- **Drum Bus** (kick, snare, hats, perc, 808) → Mix Bus
- **Bass Bus** → Mix Bus
- **Music Bus** (keys, guitars, pads, synths) → Mix Bus
- **Vocal Bus** (lead, doubles, harmonies, BGs) → Mix Bus
- **FX Bus** (parallel reverb/delay returns) → Mix Bus
- **Mix Bus** (glue comp, EQ, saturation) → Master
- **Master** (limiter, metering: LUFS, true peak, spectrum)

## 6.2 Parallel Processing (Aux Sends)
- **Drum Crush:** heavy comp (8:1–20:1, fast attack, fast release), blend 20–40%.
- **Vocal Crush:** parallel comp + saturation, blend 15–30%.
- **Bass Crush:** saturation + comp, blend 20%.
- **FX Returns:** Reverb (plate/hall/room), Delay (1/4, 1/8, 1/16 dotted), Modulation (chorus/flanger).

## 6.3 Track Order & Color Coding
1. Kick (red)
2. Snare (red)
3. Hi-Hats (red)
4. Percussion (red)
5. 808 / Sub (blue)
6. Bass (blue)
7. Keys / Piano (green)
8. Guitars (green)
9. Pads / Synths (green)
10. Lead Vocal (yellow)
11. Doubles (yellow)
12. Harmonies (yellow)
13. Background Vocals (yellow)
14. FX Returns (purple)

## 6.4 Mix Bus Chain (starting point)
- Saturation (tape, subtle, +1–2 dB drive).
- EQ (gentle: HPF 30 Hz, slight low-shelf +0.5 dB, slight high-shelf +0.5 dB).
- Compressor (SSL-style, 2:1, slow attack 30 ms, auto release, 2–4 dB GR).
- Limiter (true peak -1 dBTP, for reference only during mixing).
- Metering (LUFS, true peak, spectrum, stereo correlation).

## 6.5 Template Checklist
- [ ] Group buses created and routed.
- [ ] Parallel compression auxes set up.
- [ ] FX returns (reverb plate/hall, delay 1/4 & 1/8) created.
- [ ] Master bus chain in place.
- [ ] Color coding applied.
- [ ] Markers for verse/chorus/bridge.
- [ ] Reference track imported on muted track (level-matched).
- [ ] Monitoring: nearfields + headphones + small speaker (phone/Auratone).

# 7. HIT-SONG STRUCTURES, THEMES & PATTERNS

## 7.1 Common Song Structures

**Pop (ABABCB):** Intro (4–8, hook tease) → Verse (8–16) → Pre-Chorus (4–8, build) → Chorus (8–16, hook/title) → Verse → Pre → Chorus → Bridge (8, contrast) → Chorus → Outro. Hook within first 30 s.

**Hip-Hop:** Intro (producer tag, 4–8) → Verse (16) → Hook (8) → Verse → Hook → Bridge/Verse → Hook → Outro. Ad-libs throughout.

**Trap:** Intro (melody + 808, 4–8) → Verse (8–16) → Hook (8) → Verse → Hook → Bridge (beat switch/melody change) → Hook → Outro. Beat switches for energy.

**R&B:** Intro (vocal riff/pad) → Verse → Pre → Chorus → Verse → Pre → Chorus → Bridge (key change or breakdown) → Chorus → Outro (vamp).

**Gospel:** Intro (instrumental swell) → Verse → Chorus (call/response) → Verse → Chorus → Bridge (modulation up) → Chorus (big) → Outro (vamp, ad-libs).

**Rock:** Intro (riff) → Verse → Chorus → Verse → Chorus → Bridge/Solo → Chorus → Outro.

**AAA (Verse/Verse/Verse):** No chorus; refrain (title) at start/end of each verse. Common in folk/storytelling (Beatles, Billy Joel).

## 7.2 Hit-Song Elements
- **Title in the hook** — repeated 3+ times.
- **Hook within first 30 seconds** — don't make listeners wait.
- **Contrast:** verse (restrained, smaller range) vs. chorus (big, higher range, fuller arrangement).
- **Pre-chorus lift:** builds tension into the chorus (rising melody, added percussion, filter opens).
- **Bridge:** contrast — different chords, stripped arrangement, or key change.
- **Modulation:** up a half or whole step for final chorus (gospel/pop).
- **Drop:** remove elements before a big hit (silence → impact).
- **Ear candy:** vocal chops, ad-libs, sound effects, countermelodies.
- **Call & response:** vocals vs. instruments or backing vocals.
- **Outro vamp:** repeat hook with ad-libs and instrumental flourishes.
- **Repetition with variation:** repeat sections but change one element (add a guitar, open a filter).

## 7.3 Common Lyrical Themes (Hit Songs)
- Love / heartbreak / longing.
- Empowerment / confidence / self-worth.
- Nostalgia / memory / time.
- Party / celebration / freedom.
- Struggle → triumph / overcoming.
- Identity / authenticity / being yourself.
- Relationships / communication / trust.
- Money / success / ambition (hip-hop/trap).
- Faith / gratitude / praise (gospel).
- Social commentary / protest (rock/hip-hop).

## 7.4 Common Production Themes
- **Minor keys** for emotional/dark (trap, drill, R&B ballads).
- **Major keys** for uplifting (pop, gospel, country).
- **Modal interchange** (borrowed chords) for color.
- **Pedal point** bass for tension.
- **Syncopation** for groove (R&B, hip-hop).
- **Swing** (55–65%) for human feel (boom bap, jazz).
- **Sidechain pumping** for movement (house, pop, trap).
- **Tension & release** — build → drop → resolve.

# 8. TRICKS OF THE TRADE

## 8.1 Universal
- **Level-match** references before A/B (match LUFS, not fader).
- **Mono check:** sum to mono; fix phase issues on wide elements.
- **Small-speaker check:** phone/Auratone/earbuds — if it doesn't translate, fix.
- **HPF everything** except kick/bass/808/sub.
- **Mono sub:** keep everything below 100–120 Hz mono.
- **Parallel compression (NY):** drums/vocals, 4:1–8:1, fast attack/release, blend 20–40%.
- **Saturation:** tape on mix bus (subtle), tube on vocals/bass, transistor on drums.
- **Reverb:** plate for vocals/snare (1.0–1.5 s, pre-delay 20–40 ms); hall for gospel/strings; room for drum ambience; gated snare (80s).
- **Delay:** 1/4 or 1/8 on vocals (filtered HPF/LPF), feedback 20–30%; slapback 80–120 ms for rock vocals.
- **Stereo width:** mid-side EQ; haas delay 10–20 ms; double-tracking; keep low end mono.
- **Vocal tuning:** Auto-Tune (trap: hard, retune 0; pop/R&B: natural, retune 20–40).
- **Drum replacement:** layer samples with acoustic for consistency (rock/pop).
- **Automation:** volume rides on vocals, filter sweeps on builds, reverb throws on phrase ends.
- **Gain staging:** keep peaks around -6 to -3 dBFS per track; avoid clipping.
- **Reference in the session:** muted track, level-matched, instant A/B.
- **Rest your ears:** 10–15 min breaks every 45–60 min; reference at low volume too.

## 8.2 Genre-Specific Tricks

**G-Funk / Classic Hip-Hop:**
- One sample + live overdubs; snare reverb fills the void when kick/bass duck.
- Sub-bass mono; Moog for leads; Juno for pads.
- Vocal upfront but de-essed; backing vocals wide.

**Pop:**
- Carve 1–3 kHz on guitars for vocals; sub-group + group compress.
- Sidechain bass to kick; parallel drum comp; tom spill cleanup.
- Air shelf 10–12 kHz on vocals; de-ess 5–8 kHz.

**House:**
- Sidechain pump is the heartbeat (kick ducks bass/pads).
- Off-beat hats; open hat on "and" of 4; keep sub mono.
- Vocal chopped/stuttered; filter sweeps on builds.

**Trap/Drill:**
- Tune 808 to key; separate transient from tail; glide/portamento.
- Rolling hats with pitch variation + ghost notes + velocity ramp.
- Layered snare (acoustic + clap + rim); short plate reverb.
- Hard Auto-Tune; dark, sparse melodies.

**Boom Bap:**
- Sampled breaks (SP-1200); swing 55–65%; tight sample trimming.
- Match kick/snare lengths; parallel comp on drums.
- Filter sample (roll low end, slight high cut); sidechain sample to kick.
- Vinyl texture; acoustic one-shots.

**R&B:**
- Soft kicks; crisp snares with room reverb; hi-hat rolls that breathe.
- Stacked harmonies (3-part) panned wide; vocal chops as ear candy.
- Modern: more low/high, scooped mids (careful); sidechain bass to kick.

**Gospel:**
- Lead forward & dry; choir wide & deep (hall reverb).
- Preserve dynamics; sidechain EQ (duck accompaniment).
- Vocal sandwich panning; mid-side to widen choir.
- Modulate up for final chorus; vamp outro.

**Rock:**
- Same EQ/comp on every track (Wallace); snare transient is king.
- Aggressive midrange cuts on guitars for vocals.
- Parallel drum comp; tom spill cleanup; samples layered.
- Preserve verse/chorus dynamics; don't over-compress.

# 9. REFERENCE-TRACK A/B CHECKLIST

When comparing your mix to a reference, check:

- [ ] **Low end:** kick/bass weight and tightness (60–100 Hz); no mud (200–400 Hz).
- [ ] **Low-mid:** 200–500 Hz congestion; carve space.
- [ ] **Midrange:** 1–3 kHz presence; vocal cut-through.
- [ ] **High-mid:** 3–5 kHz harshness; de-essing.
- [ ] **Air:** 8–12 kHz brightness; not brittle.
- [ ] **Stereo width:** mono correlation; wide elements above 200 Hz.
- [ ] **Dynamics:** crest factor; not over-compressed; verse/chorus contrast.
- [ ] **Loudness:** LUFS integrated and short-term; true peak ≤ -1 dBTP.
- [ ] **Depth:** reverb/delay placement; front-to-back.
- [ ] **Translation:** phone, earbuds, car, small speaker, club.
- [ ] **Mono compatibility:** sum to mono; no phase cancellation.
- [ ] **Balance:** vocal level vs. instruments; nothing masking.

# 10. DEEP DIVE — HIT ENGINEERS

## 10.1 Jaycen Joshua
- **Approach:** aggressive — "hit them really hard so you can really feel the drums, while everything else sounds expensive." Straddles hip-hop and pop (Bieber, Beyoncé, Rosalía, FKA Twigs, Timbaland).
- **In-the-box** for instant recall; A&R wants unlimited changes. Tries to get close on first mix; if vision differs, recommends another mixer.
- **Vocal effects 100% in the box** on sends (darker green tracks); treats vocals differently per section. Reverbs: Digidesign TL Space — "Silky Gold Plate", "QRS", "Big Pan Hall" (phasey). Many effects sends.
- **God Particle** on mix bus (some double it — he doesn't endorse doubling).
- **Low-end trick:** Soothe sidechain + clip gain; slide the kick sidechain back 2–10 samples.
- **Drums/bass:** transient shaping, harmonic EQ, low-to-high transitions; sub-grouping; kick reinforced with samples.
- **Cardi B "Money" vocal chain (from MWTM):** De-Esser → RComp → SSL Channel → Fairchild 670L → P EQ; clip gain for volume automation before compression.
- **Philosophy:** serves the record; gain structure for maximum loudness without crushing dynamics.

## 10.2 Dave Pensado
- **Grammy-winning;** 200–250 songs/year at Larrabee (LA). Pensado's Place (weekly show). Started in Atlanta 70s/80s.
- **Vocal mixing:** get vocals to cut through any mix; mic technique (cardioid for home studio to avoid room bounce; MS for stereo); movement/dancing during takes; talkback always on.
- **Mixing philosophy:** "Don't confuse advice with opinion." Honest about abilities.
- **Into The Lair series:** vocal cutting, instrument levels, mixing mistakes.
- **Effects:** reverb/delay throws; parallel processing; de-essing; EQ for presence.
- **Monitoring:** works at Echo Bar Studios (North Hollywood) for recording.

## 10.3 Leslie Braithwaite
- **Grammy-winning;** from US Virgin Islands; Full Sail. Mixed Cardi B "Money", Jack Harlow & Drake, Lil Baby.
- **Drums:** enhance bass and low end; subtractive EQ, delay, echo, compression; "mixing is about making it work and getting the vibe right, more than getting things to sound perfect."
- **Vocal chain (Cardi B):** De-Esser → RComp → SSL Channel → Fairchild 670L → P EQ; clip gain; panning harmonies; delay/echo for depth.
- **Caribbean influence:** groove and feel first; vibrant, instinctive approach.

## 10.4 Young Guru
- **Jay-Z's engineer for 10+ albums** (Roc The Mic, Baseline Studios). "The Sound of New York." Grammy for Best Urban Contemporary Album (Everything Is Love, The Carters).
- **Signal chain:** Neumann U87/U67 → Avalon 737. **SSL console, old-school analog** — spreads things out; not in-the-box (unless budget requires).
- **Workflow:** doesn't mix as he records (listens to MP3 2-track too long, gets ingrained). Starts fresh from full session. Tracks at Roc The Mic, mixes at Baseline; factors 2 extra days for Jay-Z's changes (even during mastering).
- **"Run This Town" drums:** individual tracks + loop; loop treated with Waves Renaissance EQ + C4 multiband compressor.
- **Neve vs SSL:** Neve for warmth/saturation; SSL for punch. "SSL G has low headroom in mix bus; high end not sweet — work hard to make it not harsh."
- **Philosophy:** vibe over money; choosing who mixes what; facilitating the best Jay-Z.

## 10.5 Lu Diaz
- **3x Grammy;** hip-hop mixing (DJ Khaled, Pitbull, Jay-Z, Beyoncé). Waves educator.
- **Low-end golden rules:** 3 golden rules for mixing low end (see his Waves course).
- **Mixing mistakes he made early:** 6 common errors (level, EQ, compression, reverb, panning, gain staging).
- **Techniques:** side-chaining, phase reversal, limiting, multiband compression, vocal mixing, low-end control.
- **Hip-hop mixing:** vocals, low end, drums; check translation on small speakers.

## 10.6 Tony Maserati
- **NYC R&B/hip-hop sound** (90s); huge low end, smooth velvety high. Biggie, Mary J. Blige, Puff Daddy, Beyoncé (Dangerously in Love, Grammy), Usher, Robin Thicke, Black Eyed Peas.
- **Philosophy:** keep focus on the vocal; instrumentation supports the vocal and lyric idea. "R&B/hip-hop was the only place innovation was happening."
- **Clip gain:** volume automation before compression — "one of the biggest mistakes is relying only on compression to do all the heavy lifting."
- **Mixing process:** general levels first, then refine; focus on vocal throughout.

## 10.7 Andrew Scheps
- **Multi-Grammy;** Adele, Jay-Z, Metallica, RHCP, Black Sabbath, Green Day, U2, Ziggy Marley.
- **Mixing in the box;** Scheps Omni Channel (Waves): compression flavors, preamp saturation, EQ, de-essing, parallel processing.
- **"Lighter on its feet"** (Rick Rubin note) — low end keeps a mix from being light; learn to listen to rhythmic drive.
- **Philosophy:** same settings won't replicate a mix — "the one thing missing is the person's brain." Respect the production as tracked; don't over-change.
- **Techniques:** lead guitar treatment; natural reverb; creative panning; mixing a band; distance via binaural/immersive (Atmos).
- **Adele:** vocal super loud, in the room with you; extremely difficult to balance.

## 10.8 Mike Elizondo
- **Dre protégé;** bassist/keyboardist/guitarist. In Da Club, Just Lose It, Family Affair, The Real Slim Shady, 2001, Eminem Show.
- **Workflow:** 10–15 ideas per session recorded to stereo (DAT/CD-R) as quick sketches; bass + keys + Dre; separate sounds later if needed.
- **Multi-instrumentalist** hearing: wraps around drums, vocal, harmony; production sense from bass perspective.
- **Career:** session musician (The Firm, 1996) → songwriter/producer → Aftermath core team for 11 years → producer (Fiona Apple, Avenged Sevenfold, Twenty One Pilots, Maroon 5).

# 11. ICONIC ALBUMS & SOUNDS

## 11.1 D'Angelo — *Voodoo* (Russell Elevado)
- **All analog:** tape, vintage gear, no digital. Neve saturation for warmth.
- **Varispeed:** harmonies recorded at slower speed to change timbre; triple each note, 4 parts overlapping; D'Angelo punches in new harmonies.
- **Guitar:** 3 bass strings + 5 guitar strings on one guitar.
- **Drums:** 2 on snare, 2 on kick, 1 on toms; overheads + room; 30 Hz to top; combination of close + room mics.
- **Mic technique:** stereo mic or X-Y; high-quality mic + preamp + converters.
- **Mixing:** mix in mono at certain point; take mix home, play in car 2–3 times before next day; additive or subtractive EQ.
- **Reverb:** EMT 140 plate + 2 live chambers (attics) + Echoplex.
- **Vocal riding:** fader riding during tracking (singing along in your mind).
- **Philosophy:** "making it work and getting the vibe right."

## 11.2 Steely Dan — *Aja* (Bill Schnee / Elliot Scheiner)
- **Tracking:** Producers' Workshop (Langevin AM16 preamps, EQ251 passive EQ, Stephens 24-track @30ips, no Dolby, Scotch tape). Monitoring: Altec 604 coaxial. No drum compression (clean capture).
- **Drum setup:** minimal — kick, snare, hat, toms, overheads for cymbals; no room mics (everyone in room); submixed to stereo pair except kick/snare/hat. One shared headphone mix; Schnee walks into live room to adjust.
- **Instruments:** piano stereo-miked; Rhodes stereo DI; Larry Carlton's Fender 5C3 Tweed Deluxe single mic, baffled from drums; Chuck Rainey's P-Bass flatwounds straight into LA-2A; LA-2A on Carlton's amp. Victor Feldman's electric piano solo cut live.
- **Overdubs:** Village Recorder (Feldman perc/keys, Wayne Shorter sax, Becker/Fagen/Schmit/Dias).
- **Mixing:** Schnee (performance mixes, no automation) → withdrew after a day; Scheiner mixed with NECAM moving-fader automation, 3-day process, countless passes. Bass-missing incident: manual mute not engaged; recalled mix with bass loud; restarted.
- **Personnel:** 30 musicians, 7 songs, 6 studios (LA + NY); multiple drummers/bands per song; Rainey on bass (except Deacon Blues, Becker). Steve Gadd's first-take drum solo on title track.
- **Hi-fi demo record** until Fagen's *The Nightfly* (1982). All four engineers shared Grammy for best engineered recording.

## 11.3 Motown Sound
- **3-track workflow:** track 1 rhythm, track 2 strings/horns, track 3 vocals; bounce between machines (rhythm bounced 4x, brass 2x, strings 1x). Submixing = irreversible decisions.
- **Gear:** Pultecs, Fairchild 660, LA-2A, Neumann 67/47, RCA 44/77, EMT 140 plate, 2 live chambers (attics), Echoplex. Motown EQ (Mike McLean DIY Langevin graphic), Motown DI box.
- **Lawrence Horn's parallel compression:** split vocal to 2 channels — (1) natural (light EQ + reverb, set at correct level) + (2) heavily compressed (high ratio, low threshold, medium attack/release, +5dB+ at 5–8kHz, roll off >12kHz, **no makeup gain**); blend compressed under natural. Barry Gordy hated limiters; parallel comp kept natural feel.
- **Disk cutting:** HPF 70 Hz (brickwall); boost 100/200/300 Hz for bass definition; energy 2–15 kHz; "sonic cliff" (don't distort/skip); level drops near inner grooves up to 2.5 dB.
- **Mic technique:** fader riding during tracking; look singer in the eye; sing along. Punching in from film dubbing; channel mutes; arrangement on board.
- **Monitoring:** 3 speakers for 3 tracks (mono) until 1964, then stereo + 8-track (DIY, no commercial 8-tracks available).
- **Tape saturation** = organic compression; tube/transformer saturation. Reverb: attic chambers + EMT 140 + Echoplex.

## 11.4 DJ Premier
- **Boom bap:** chopped samples, layering drums, precise sample selection; scratches as instrument.
- **Sampling:** cut by notes, rearrange; drum breaks (Impeach the President, Amen Brother); layer drum samples for punch.
- **Bassline:** from sample or added (Moog-style).
- **Mixing:** focus on kick/snare; sample EQ (roll low end, slight high cut); sidechain sample to kick; swing via MPC.
- **Scratches panned;** vocal samples as hooks; "Kick In The Door" from Screamin' Jay Hawkins.

## 11.5 J Dilla
- **MPC3000 (96PPQ);** shift timing; independent swing per track. "Dilla Time": juxtaposition of straight and swing simultaneously — snare rushed ~18ms, kick rushed ~10ms, hi-hat straighter at 51% swing, bass dragged.
- **Micro-chopping (eighth-note technique);** drums extracted from samples and rearranged.
- **Recording:** Neve 8078 tracking; SSL mixing (E-series with G upgrades).
- **Outboard:** DBX 160X on vocals; Yamaha SPX900 "Symphonic" program with D-filter for watery/bubbly texture.
- **Kick:** old oscillator triggered by gate at ~40 Hz, short, no length (sub below kick).
- **Mixing (Bob Power):** high/low-pass everything; keep "kaka" out; SSL G mix bus (low headroom, work bottom hard, high end not sweet). Neve EQ saturation for warmth.
- **David Kennedy:** post-mix digital editing (Sound Forge), sequencing; Neve fan.
- **Tom Coyne mastering:** "not much to do other than balance levels" (already well-balanced).
- **Donuts:** micro-chopped samples; drums from original sample rearranged.

# 12. DEEP VOCAL SETTINGS PER GENRE

## 12.1 Pop (modern — Stent/Joshua style)
- HPF 80–100 Hz; de-ess 5–8 kHz (2–4 dB); comp 2:1–3:1; saturation; air shelf 10–12 kHz (+2 dB).
- Reverb: plate, pre-delay 20–40 ms, 1.2–1.5 s; delay 1/8 or 1/4 (filtered).
- Doubles/harmonies panned 80% L/R; lead center; clip gain before compression; in-the-box recall.

## 12.2 R&B (Maserati style)
- Huge low-end foundation; vocal clear and present.
- **Parallel comp:** natural channel + heavily compressed (8–10 dB GR, 4:1, soft knee, +5 dB at 5–8 kHz, no makeup gain) blended.
- Reverb: plate/hall, pre-delay 30–50 ms; delay throws; saturation (tube).
- Harmonies stacked (3-part), panned wide; vocal chops as ear candy.

## 12.3 Hip-Hop (Young Guru / Lu Diaz)
- Lead vocal upfront, de-essed; Auto-Tune (natural to hard). Doubles panned L/R; ad-libs panned; harmonies.
- Reverb: short plate or room; delay 1/8 (filtered). Sidechain/ducking for clarity; HPF 100 Hz. Clip gain; R-Vox style.

## 12.4 Gospel
- Lead forward and dry; choir wide and deep (hall reverb). Parallel comp light (preserve dynamics); sidechain EQ (duck accompaniment).
- Vocal sandwich: lead center, doubles/harmonies hard L/R; mid-side EQ/compression to widen choir.
- Reverb: hall 2.0–3.0 s, pre-delay 40–60 ms; big tails. Minimal de-essing; natural tone; subtle saturation.

## 12.5 Rock (Wallace / Scheps style)
- Lead upfront; de-ess; comp 3:1–4:1; saturation. Slapback delay 80–120 ms (optional); plate reverb 1.0–1.5 s.
- Doubles for width; harmonies panned. Carve 1–3 kHz on guitars for vocal space. Air shelf 10–12 kHz; HPF 100 Hz.

## 12.6 Trap / Drill
- Hard Auto-Tune (retune speed 0). Lead often drenched in reverb/delay (ad-libs). Doubles panned; ad-libs panned wide.
- HPF 100–150 Hz; aggressive de-ess; saturation. Reverb: hall/plate 1.5–2.5 s; delay 1/4 with feedback. Vocal as instrument; stacked harmonies.

## 12.7 House
- Vocal chopped/stuttered; filtered (LPF sweeps). Reverb: plate 1.0–1.5 s; delay 1/8 dotted. De-ess; HPF 100 Hz; sidechain to kick. Doubles panned; wide stereo. Vocoder/chops.

## 12.8 Motown (classic)
- Lawrence Horn parallel comp: natural + heavily compressed (8–10 dB GR, 4:1, soft knee, +5 dB at 5–8 kHz, roll >12 kHz, no makeup gain).
- Reverb: attic chamber + EMT 140 plate + Echoplex. Fader riding during tracking; minimal comp while tracking. Saturation from tube/transformer/tape. Mono mix; 3-track workflow.
